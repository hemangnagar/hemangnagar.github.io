1/ I asked Claude to argue against four decisions, then didn't let it decide any of them.

Decision Gate: one model states what a decision rests on, a second attacks each claim, and three lines of deterministic code return ACT / WAIT / ABANDON.

Open source. 🧵
---
2/ The hospital case, replacing pagers with a messaging app, came back ABANDON on one argument:

Pagers run on a separate radio network. An app that *replaces* them removes the fallback for exactly the outages where the app is down.

"Replace" is a different decision from "add."
---
3/ Then the ledger computes the counterfactual: resolve that FATAL argument and the gate still says WAIT. Ten BLOCKING problems remain.

That line separates "the framing is wrong" from "we need more evidence." It's computed by a rule, not narrated by a model.
---
4/ The Spark→DuckDB migration came back WAIT. WAIT isn't no.

9 blocking challenges, each with the measurement that retires it: replay the 5 heaviest jobs at real concurrency, measure bytes actually read, cost a tuned-Spark baseline, plot the thread-scaling curve.

Do them → ACT, carrying 13 named risks.
---
5/ Two findings I'm publishing, not hiding:

• In 3 rounds the adversary never ran out of objections. The round budget stopped the review, not convergence.
• Across 92 challenges it rated ZERO as non-blocking.

The model is one step back from the decision, not removed from it.
---
6/ Every ledger in the repo is unedited and re-gates from the file: `decision-gate gate ledger.json` recomputes the action without calling a model.
