One-pager · pharmacy & health-plan evidence

One engine, three possible markets.

The engine turns a pharmacy's or a health plan's own records into a claim-level fact pack where every dollar traces to a source row. Which market it serves first depends on which door opens — and that is where your contacts decide it.

As of September 21, 2026 · Hemang Nagar

What the engine does

It is evidence infrastructure, not a detection tool: it says what the record shows, reproducibly, and cannot invent a number.

  • Every figure traces to a source row. Claims, remittances, contracts and price benchmarks go in; each computed dollar carries the IDs of the records it came from, so a hostile expert or an auditor can re-trace it.
  • Same inputs, same output, every time. The calculation is deterministic and replayable, which is what a testifying expert or a fiduciary needs to defend a method as their own.
  • The narrative cannot state a dollar that is not in the fact pack. An AI writes the plain-English summary, but a gate rejects any amount not already computed.
Working demo on synthetic data — Medicare Maximum Fair Price refund reconciliation: hemangnagar.dev/rxguard-mfp
The MFP case is one join. The same engine runs against NADAC, PBM contract guarantees, or 340B rebates.

Three markets, one engine

The pharmacy ledger is the one we can start on now; the ERISA file is the biggest market; 340B has the nearest deadline. The right first market is the one where someone who already holds the data will run a paid pilot.

Where your help decides it

Five answers, and the first market picks itself.

  1. Pharmacy litigation. Do you know plaintiffs' counsel on the Arkansas Express Scripts case, or anyone at NCPA who can say how TRUST LLC collects assignor pharmacies' claim data today, and what it costs per pharmacy?
  2. Audit defense. When your pharmacy was audited, who reconstructed the records, and what did the firm bill for that part? Would a firm you know pay a fixed fee for a 20-pharmacy pilot on a live matter?
  3. ERISA. Do you know a fee-only benefits consultant, a PBM auditor, or an ERISA lawyer who would let us run one employer's claims extract under a BAA? That one file decides whether the ERISA market is reachable before 2029.
  4. 340B. Is anyone in your network in hospital pharmacy or 340B compliance?
  5. The product itself. As a lawyer: would a machine-generated, reproducible, source-cited file change how you would defend or bring a case — or is it a nice-to-have next to what paralegals already produce?

What we are not asking for: money, or an introduction to any pharmacy that has not asked for one.
